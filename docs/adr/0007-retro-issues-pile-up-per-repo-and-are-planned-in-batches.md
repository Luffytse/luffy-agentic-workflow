# Retro issues pile up per repo and are planned in batches

`retro` used to hand its suggestions to the person in its report, where most were read once and lost. Now every suggestion except a repeated Bug class becomes a **Retro issue** on a GitHub tracker, and the open ones form that repo's **Retro backlog**. A **Retro planning** session, run by the new `retro-planning` skill, works through one repo's backlog in a batch: it merges duplicates, closes what was seen only once, and turns at most 3 groups into Tickets. We chose this because one thread's retro sees one session, so it cannot tell a one-off from a pattern; only a backlog that collects "seen again" votes across threads can. `dev-skills` is loaded by several projects, so no skill text names one project or repo, and a Retro issue goes to the repo where its fix lands.

## Filing (what `retro` does)

- A repeated Bug class from `agentic-review` still becomes a check or a `REVIEW.md` line straight away, in the retro's own pull request ([ADR 0003](0003-code-review-runs-find-verify-rank-in-rounds.md)). Every other suggestion becomes a Retro issue: label `retro`, title starting `Retro:`, and **no** `needs-triage`, so build threads ignore it and `triage` skips it (see Consequences). Its body names the source thread, what went wrong, and the suggested fix. `retro` creates the `retro` label if the repo lacks it.
- **Routing follows where the fix lands.** A problem with a skill or the workflow (skill text, review rules, how threads run) goes to the `dev-skills` repo, from any project. A problem only one project has (its CI, its docs, its `CLAUDE.md`) goes to that project's repo. No suggestion goes to both. A project that wants a skill of its own builds it in its own repo or `CLAUDE.md`; it may still suggest a `dev-skills` change, which `dev-skills`' Retro planning accepts or closes as won't do, with a reason.
- `retro` finds the `dev-skills` repo from the `repository` field of the plugin's manifest (`plugins/dev-skills/.claude-plugin/plugin.json`), and the project's tracker from its `docs/agents/issue-tracker.md`. When the thread cannot write to the `dev-skills` repo, `retro` puts the issue text in its report so the coordinator can file it.
- **Votes.** When an open Retro issue already covers a suggestion, `retro` comments "seen again in <thread>" on it instead of opening a duplicate.
- **Trigger.** After filing, `retro` counts the repo's open Retro issues and reads the newest closed Retro planning record. Its report says Retro planning is due when 10 or more are open, or when 2 weeks have passed since the last planning and at least 4 are open. The numbers live in one place in the skill. The person decides when to start; the coordinator offers a thread.
- `implement` and `implement-spec` run `retro` on their own thread as their last step, once the pull request is merged and its issue is closed; a thread that handed its pull request to the person waits, and runs `retro` after the person merges it, or asks whether to run it if the person drops or closes the pull request unmerged, or the thread never opened one. Planning threads keep their project's rule instead.

## Retro planning (what `retro-planning` does)

It runs per repo and reads only that repo's Retro issues; the `dev-skills` repo is planned in the project that builds it.

1. **Snapshot and open record.** At its start the planning files its record, an **open** issue titled `Retro planning <yyyy-mm-dd>`, label `retro-planning`, and reads every open Retro issue with its "seen again" votes and its age as of the record's creation. That snapshot is all it plans. Anything filed or voted on later waits for the next planning and is listed under "Arrived during this planning"; other threads keep running and nothing is held. A late vote is not lost: it counts as a new vote next time.
2. Group them by root cause and merge duplicates.
3. Propose a verdict per group. Seen in only one thread: close as `wontfix` with a reason, unless it is severe (lost data, security, a large waste). Seen in 2 or more threads: act on it.
4. Act on at most 3 groups. Each becomes a `ready-for-agent` Ticket with a done condition, linking the Retro issues it absorbs; those issues close when the Ticket closes as completed, and return to the backlog with their votes if it closes as not planned (or is abandoned). Prefer a check or hook over a new prose rule. A repeated group over the cap stays open and comes next at the next planning, after any group from step 8. Only the person may raise the cap, and only for that planning; the record notes the cap used and the reason.
5. **One item at a time.** The planning walks the person through the groups in order, the `wontfix` ones included, each with full details: how the workflow works today, the incident in each thread, the shared root cause, the vote count, the proposed verdict and reason, and whether to address the problem if it acts. It then invokes the complete `to-tickets` workflow to investigate, draft and obtain approval of the concrete solution before publication. A problem approval alone does not approve that solution. Several required Tickets return before publication for a spin-off decision. The planning carries out each call at once, then reconciles returned identifiers, source mappings and incomplete actions into the record before showing another group. Real Tickets count toward the cap even after partial failure; resume repairs their publication without duplicating them or discarding a declined solution. Closing or spinning off a proposed Ticket frees its slot for the next acting group; once the cap is full, each later acting group is shown, still one at a time, as held back. The mechanical closes (expiries, and issues whose Ticket closed as completed) come in one short list at the end.
6. An issue with no new vote across 2 plannings is closed with "reopen if seen again", unless it was held back only by the cap or is spun off.
7. **Spin-off.** A group the person wants to discuss further is not decided in this planning. It gets a separate planning thread (asked of the coordinator in a project, otherwise of the person), and each of its issues a comment naming that thread. A spun-off group does not count toward the cap, stays open and cannot expire. The owning thread ends by giving each issue a verdict: absorbed by a Ticket it files, or closed with a reason. It files no record of its own and leaves the spinning planning's record body alone; it comments each Ticket it files on that record, and later plannings read those comments with the record and recheck those fixes like any other. Until then, later plannings list those issues as "spun off, waiting" and skip them, as they skip issues waiting on an open Ticket.
8. Recheck whether the problem behind an earlier fix has come back. If it has, the fix did not work, and that group goes first.
9. The record lists the snapshot, the cap, every verdict and vote count, what was spun off and what arrived during the planning. The planning closes it at the end. A planning that pauses leaves it open and is resumed from it: the resumed run keeps the snapshot, first brings the record up to date with what was already done on the issues (closes, Tickets, spin-offs), redoes the grouping for the issues still without a verdict, and walks on from there. The next planning, and `retro`'s 2-week clock, read only closed records; the clock runs from the newest closed record's creation, which is now its planning's start.

The thresholds (10, 2 weeks with 4, at most 3 groups, 2 plannings) are the user's choices, not taken from a source. Inside a Milestone (ADR 0004, ADR 0008) the 3-group cap and the walk with the person do not apply: agents decide every group at Release end (see `retro-planning`).

## Considered options

- **Keep suggestions in the report only.** Nothing tracks them, and no thread can see that two others hit the same problem.
- **File them as `needs-triage` issues.** `triage` and build threads would pick them up one at a time and act on one-offs; Retro issues need batch judgement across threads, which is what Retro planning is for.
- **Show one list of every verdict and approve it at once.** The first planning did this, and the person found every group, verdict and Ticket draft too much to judge together; one item at a time, acting on each call, keeps each judgement small, and the open record lets a long planning pause and resume.
- **Act on every repeated suggestion.** A planning that turns many groups into Tickets crowds out feature work; a limit of 3 keeps the fixes small and makes each planning choose.
- **One shared backlog for every project.** Routing by where the fix lands keeps each repo's backlog about things that repo can change, and keeps `dev-skills` free of any one project's problems.

## Consequences

- `retro` gains filing, routing, votes and the trigger (Patches). `plugin.json` gains a `repository` field.
- A new skill of our own, `retro-planning`, lives under `plugins/dev-skills/skills/`, and `ask-matt` routes to it (a Patch).
- `triage` must skip issues labelled `retro`, since they carry no `needs-triage` and would otherwise look untriaged (a Patch).
- `implement` and `implement-spec` end with `retro` (Patches).
- This repo gets a CI job running `scripts/check-plugin.sh` and `claude plugin validate .` on every pull request.
